Campus Guiding & Giving Directions
🎯 Target Grammar Structure: Giving Directions & Explaining Facilities using Prepositions of Place & Movement + First Conditional / Modal Verbs — Use phrases like "across from", "next to", "on the second floor", and patterns like "If you walk down this corridor, you will see..." or "Here is where you can..." to give polite, clear directions to visitors on campus.

🎮 Level-Up: Vocabulary Frames

Master these 5 key campus guiding words and complete the sentence frames below.

Facility
A specific room, building, or special area provided for a particular activity or school service.
Example: Our new sports gym is the most modern ________ on campus.
Courtyard
An open outdoor space surrounded by school buildings where students gather between classes.
Example: Students enjoy sitting under the trees in the center ________ during break time.
Laboratory
A room specially equipped for scientific experiments and practical student research.
Example: We wear protective coats whenever we work in the chemistry ________.
Auditorium
A large hall with seats where the entire school gathers for speeches, assemblies, and concerts.
Example: The welcome ceremony for our visiting guests will be held in the main ________.
Canteen
The large school dining hall where students and teachers eat lunch together every day.
Example: After fourth period, everyone walks quickly to the ________ to get hot dumplings and soup.

🗝️ Teacher Key

Comprehension Answers:
1. Leo is an exchange student visiting from London, UK for his exchange week.
2. The ambassadors will show him the classroom building, courtyard, science/computer labs, canteen, library, and sports field.
3. Prepositions of place (next to, opposite, across from, on the second floor) and directional conditionals (If you walk past..., you will see...).

Grammar Spotlight Answers (Directions & Prepositions of Place):
1. "The library is in the second floor." ➔ "The library is on the second floor." (Use 'on' for building floors: on the ground/first/second floor).
2. "If you will turn left, you see the lab." ➔ "If you turn left, you will see the lab." (First conditional 'if-clause' takes present simple, result takes 'will').
3. "The canteen is opposite to the gym." ➔ "The canteen is opposite the gym." or "The canteen is across from the gym." ('Opposite' does not take 'to' in standard modern English).
4. "Can students to play ping pong here?" ➔ "Can students play ping pong here?" (Modal auxiliary 'can' takes bare infinitive without 'to').
